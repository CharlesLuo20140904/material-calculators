Why Project Matter exists

Material shopping often begins with a simple measurement and ends with a product sold by the gallon, box, roll, panel, or bag. Generic area formulas do not explain that final step. Project Matter connects the measurement to the purchasing unit and shows the assumptions in between.

The six calculators cover paint, tile, wallpaper, flooring, drywall, and small concrete placements. Each tool runs locally in the browser, keeps measurement inputs on the device, supports imperial and metric units where relevant, and rounds to the purchase increment described beside the result.

Who maintains the site

Project Matter is independently published and maintained through the public material-calculators repository. The site does not represent a manufacturer, retailer, contractor, engineering practice, or standards organization.

Articles are credited to Project Matter because they document the site’s own calculation methods and purchasing workflow. We do not use invented expert profiles, stock biographies, or credentials. Source links point to the manufacturer, trade association, retailer, or product document used to check a practical claim.

Four publishing principles

Show the math

A result should be reproducible from the measurements, formula, allowance, and package size shown on the page.

Name the assumption

Defaults are starting points. Product labels, drawings, local requirements, and supplier quotes take priority.

Use primary sources

Coverage, yield, and installation guidance are checked against current manufacturer or industry material when available.

Correct in public

Source code and issue history provide a visible route for corrections instead of silently changing a disputed formula.

Where an estimate stops

These tools estimate purchase quantities. They do not assess structural adequacy, code compliance, substrate condition, moisture, hazardous materials, fire ratings, load paths, mix design, installation safety, or whether a product is suitable for a particular building.

Confirm the current product label and package coverage before ordering. For structural concrete, fire-rated assemblies, wet-area systems, hazardous coatings, damaged substrates, or work requiring permits, use the project documents and a qualified local professional.
