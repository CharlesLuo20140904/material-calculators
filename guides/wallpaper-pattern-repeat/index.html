Wallpaper planning guide

Pattern repeat, without the guesswork.

The room has not changed—but a straight or half-drop match can change how much usable paper comes from every roll. Here is how to read the label and estimate matched drops.

Short answerPatterned paper is calculated by full matched drops, not total square footage.

Three match types, three cutting plans

The match symbol tells you how adjacent strips align. It affects cutting before the first strip reaches the wall, so it belongs in the estimate—not as a last-minute waste percentage.

Random or free match

A random match does not require a motif to meet at the seam. Each drop needs the wall height plus enough trimming allowance for the ceiling and baseboard. Small textures, vertical stripes, and some grasscloths may use this match, but always follow the product label.

Straight match

On a straight match, the same part of the design meets at the same height on every strip. The cut length is rounded up to the next full vertical repeat. A little extra wall height can therefore consume an entire additional repeat.

Half-drop or offset match

On a half-drop match, every other strip is shifted vertically, commonly by half the repeat. The Wallcoverings Association notes that the design repeats horizontally after three strips. For planning, alternate strips may need a longer cut, so this match generally produces the most waste.

The matched-drop formula

First convert wall height, trim, and repeat to the same unit. Then round the raw drop upward. A straight-match planning formula is:

Straight matchMatched drop = ceiling((wall height + trim) ÷ repeat) × repeat

Drops per roll = floor(packaged roll length ÷ matched drop)

Count drops for each wall separately: wall width divided by roll width, rounded up. This matters because a half-width strip left at one wall does not automatically become a full-height strip on the next wall. Finally, divide total drops by drops per roll and round up.

Do not subtract ordinary doors and windows automatically.

You normally still cut strips for the wall above and below those openings. Deduct only a full-height opening that truly eliminates complete drops.

Worked example: 20.5-inch repeat

Suppose the wall is 8 feet high. Add 4 inches total trim, giving a raw drop of 100 inches. The paper has a 20.5-inch straight repeat and comes on a 33-foot roll.

  1. 100 ÷ 20.5 = 4.88 repeats.
  2. Round up to 5 repeats.
  3. 5 × 20.5 = 102.5 inches, or about 8.54 feet, per matched drop.
  4. 33 ÷ 8.54 = 3 full drops per roll.

A square-foot calculation misses the 2.5 inches discarded for pattern alignment on every strip. The difference becomes larger when the repeat is long, the wall height falls just above a repeat boundary, or the paper uses a half-drop match.

Four mistakes that cause short orders

  • Using a catalog roll name. “Single” and “double” terminology does not replace the physical width and length printed on the package.
  • Entering horizontal repeat. The cutting calculation needs the vertical repeat.
  • Combining all wall widths too early. Count strips wall by wall so corners are handled realistically.
  • Ignoring run numbers. Order the project together and keep one dye lot. A replacement roll can vary slightly in color.

For removable papers, murals, reverse-hang products, or papers sold as panels, follow the manufacturer’s installation layout instead of treating the package as a conventional roll.

Sources and limits

Match definitions are based on the Wallcoverings Association pattern-match guide. Product symbols and installation instructions control when they differ. This guide estimates material; it does not replace an installer’s cutting plan.
