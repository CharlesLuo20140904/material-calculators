Paint planning guide

Coverage is a product input, not a universal constant.

A 350 ft²-per-gallon default is a useful starting point. Surface texture, porosity, tools, film build, color change, and the specific coating determine what the can can actually cover.

Best practiceUse the current label or product data page for the paint you will buy.

Start with the specified spread rate

Coverage expresses the area one unit of coating is expected to cover under stated conditions. Sherwin-Williams gives about 350–400 ft² as a typical gallon range while warning that application method and surface condition change requirements. Its technical FAQ also calls 400 ft² theoretical because substrate texture and paint retained by tools are not included.

Basic quantityCoated area ÷ product coverage = exact paint volume

Coated area already includes the number of coats and any selected allowance.

A lower coverage value produces a more conservative estimate. That does not make it a substitute for the product data sheet. Masonry coatings, primers, specialty finishes, stains, textured products, and high-build coatings may use very different rates.

One coat and two coats are different projects

The calculator multiplies net surface area by coat count. Two coats therefore require twice the theoretical film area, even when the second coat feels faster to apply. Manufacturer directions, the existing color and condition, the new color, and the required finish determine the actual coating system.

ConditionPlanning response
Same color, sound surfaceFollow the product’s stated coat system; do not assume one coat from appearance alone
Major light-to-dark changeExpect coverage and hide to influence coat count
New porous drywallPrice primer separately, then finish coats
Rough or textured wallUse the label range conservatively and allow for extra surface area

Keep primer out of the finish-paint total

Primer is a separate product with its own spread rate, number of coats, and package sizes. Sherwin-Williams gives a general primer range of about 200–300 ft² per gallon, lower than its typical finish-paint range. A project that needs one primer coat and two finish coats should be estimated as two purchases, not three identical coats.

Surface preparation is not a volume adjustment.

Cleaning, repair, sanding, hazardous coating controls, drying, and compatible primers must be determined before the quantity estimate is treated as a purchase plan.

Use allowance for uncertainty—not to hide a wrong input

The extra percentage can cover roller and brush retention, small measurement errors, touch-up, and a limited reserve. It should not compensate for omitting a coat, using the wrong coverage, or failing to measure an unusually large opening.

Can-size rounding creates additional overage. If the exact result is 2.03 gallons and the product is sold in gallons and quarts, the practical combination may be 2.25 gallons. If only gallon cans are available, the purchase becomes 3 gallons. Check whether partially used custom-tinted paint is returnable before intentionally oversizing the order.

Five inputs to confirm at the shelf

  1. Product name and intended substrate.
  2. Published coverage or spread-rate range.
  3. Required primer and coat system.
  4. Available can sizes for the chosen base and color.
  5. Application, drying, ventilation, and safety instructions.

Record the product and color formula for future touch-up. When several cans form one finish coat, follow the manufacturer or retailer’s guidance on mixing containers for color consistency.

Sources and limits

Coverage ranges and caveats are based on the Sherwin-Williams paint calculator FAQ and painting FAQ. Product-specific instructions control. This guide estimates quantity and does not establish coating compatibility or a safe work procedure.
