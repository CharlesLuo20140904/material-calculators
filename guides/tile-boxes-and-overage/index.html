Tile worked example

Count the pieces. Then respect the box.

A 12 × 10 foot surface using 12 × 24 inch tile, 10% waste, and eight pieces per box requires nine full boxes—not 7.5 boxes and not merely 66 loose tiles.

Example result9 boxes · 72 tiles · 144 ft² supplied

Measure the tiled surface

For a rectangular floor, multiply finished length by finished width. A 12 × 10 foot room is 120 ft². For walls, measure each rectangular field and add the areas. Deduct only openings that truly eliminate tile, and separately measure niches, returns, tub aprons, or other surfaces that receive tile.

Keep all dimensions in one unit before dividing by the tile area. Grout-joint width affects layout and cut sizes but is not normally used to shrink the material count; the waste allowance covers planning uncertainty and cuts.

Convert area to whole tile pieces

A 12 × 24 inch tile covers 288 in², which is 2 ft². The 120 ft² surface therefore needs 60 uncut tile equivalents. Apply the selected 10% allowance before rounding:

Piece count(120 ft² ÷ 2 ft²) × 1.10 = 66 tiles

The result is already a whole number. Any fractional result would round upward to the next tile.

Ten percent is a planning starting point, not a promise. Daltile recommends approximately 10% additional tile and notes the value of keeping matching attic stock for future repair. Pattern, room shape, breakage, sorting, and layout can require more.

Round again at the carton

If the selected carton contains eight tiles, 66 ÷ 8 = 8.25 boxes. Since a partial carton is not the assumed buying unit, round upward to nine. Nine boxes supply 72 tiles or 144 ft².

A 10% setting can produce more than 10% purchased overage.

Whole-box rounding is the reason. On small rooms and large cartons, the extra step is material.

Verify the real carton before ordering

  • Use the square-foot coverage and piece count printed for the exact SKU.
  • Confirm that cartons from one dye lot or production lot are available.
  • Inspect the recommended layout and minimum cut guidance before fixing waste.
  • Ask about return rules for unopened cartons and keep suitable matching attic stock.
  • Do not use nominal tile dimensions when the manufacturer lists actual dimensions that change the count.

Source and scope

The 10% planning reference is supported by the Daltile tile FAQ. The worked example is original arithmetic using Project Matter’s formula. Manufacturer instructions, layout drawings, substrate requirements, movement joints, mortar coverage, and safety requirements remain outside this quantity estimate.
