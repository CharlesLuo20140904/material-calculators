Tile planning guide

The waste percentage begins on the layout drawing.

Ten percent is a common baseline, but a centered grid, diagonal field, herringbone pattern, narrow room, veined stone, or large-format tile can change the number of usable cuts.

Planning ruleChoose an allowance after the dry layout—not before measuring.

What the 10% baseline actually means

Daltile recommends purchasing approximately 10% more tile than the anticipated project quantity and keeping additional matching tile for repairs. Lowe’s tile calculator similarly suggests an extra 10% for trim and waste. Those recommendations are useful starting points, not guarantees that every cut plan fits inside that allowance.

The percentage covers material that does not become a full visible tile: perimeter cuts, broken pieces, chips, color or pattern sorting, trial cuts, and a limited reserve. It does not correct an inaccurate room measurement or replace the actual number of pieces in a carton.

Five conditions that change usable yield

ConditionWhy it changes waste
Straight grid in a regular roomOffcuts may continue on the opposite side when dimensions cooperate
Diagonal or herringbone fieldMore perimeter cuts and directional offcuts
Niches, returns, columns, or many doorsMore separate cut pieces and fewer reusable remnants
Large-format or fragile tileOne failed cut represents more area and may be harder to reuse
Strong variation or vein directionLayout selection can remove otherwise sound pieces from the field

A percentage alone cannot model every pattern. For a demanding layout, draw the grid at the real tile and grout-joint dimensions, count full and cut pieces, then use the percentage as an additional contingency rather than the only plan.

A defensible way to set the allowance

  1. Measure each tiled surface separately and add the areas.
  2. Confirm actual tile dimensions, joint width, orientation, and pattern.
  3. Place control lines so opposite cuts are intentional and small slivers are avoided.
  4. Count the pieces needed by the layout where practical.
  5. Add breakage and repair stock based on tile availability and project risk.
  6. Convert the final piece count to whole cartons and inspect the actual overage after rounding.
Do not promise a fixed “diagonal percentage” without the drawing.

The room-to-tile relationship controls whether offcuts are reusable. Two rooms of equal area can consume different piece counts.

Separate installation waste from future repair stock

Installation waste is consumed during layout and cutting. Attic stock remains unopened or stored after completion for future replacement. Combining both into one percentage can be acceptable for a small estimate, but the order decision is clearer when each purpose is stated.

Matching tile can become difficult to obtain later because production lots, quarry variation, color, calibration, finish, and SKU availability change. Store retained pieces flat and protected, label the product and lot, and keep the purchase record. Confirm whether unopened cartons may be returned before ordering a large reserve.

Sources and limits

General 10% guidance and attic-stock context come from the Daltile FAQ and Lowe’s tile calculator guidance. The installer and current product instructions should set the final order. This guide does not design the substrate, waterproofing, movement joints, mortar, grout, or installation method.
